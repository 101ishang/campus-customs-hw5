# Campus Customs desk: dashboard design

The dashboard is designed as a calm operations desk rather than a generic admin screen. A dark teal rail anchors the left side with the Campus Customs mark, live connection health, navigation, and the desk operator identity. The main canvas uses a light paper-like background, generous spacing, and a short “A calmer way to run the desk” welcome so the page feels approachable during a busy support shift.

The top summary row gives the operator three quick signals: open versus resolved tickets, the current checking balance, and the five-agent crew connection. The queue sits beside the selected-ticket panel. Each ticket has a small visual glyph, requester, subject, and an Open or Resolved status, so choosing the next piece of work takes one click. The detail panel keeps the run control beside the ticket identity and shows the request facts before the team starts.

The five agents read differently at a glance through a consistent card layout and role-specific colors: coral for Boss, blue for Inventory, gold for Accounting, mint for Facilities, and violet for Customer Service. Each card shows the agent’s remit, a live or standby state, the latest sentence they said, and the MCP tool chip when a tool was used. During a run, the card gains a soft green focus ring and the activity feed below records delegation, agent updates, and MCP calls in time order. This makes the team’s reasoning inspectable without forcing the operator to read raw logs.

Resolved work is visible rather than disappearing. A resolved ticket keeps its place in the queue with a green status pill, the selected-request header repeats that state, and the summary card changes to “Run complete · ready to review.” The open/resolved counts in the summary row update with the board, giving the operator a quick sense of progress.

Cash is deliberately treated as a human-controlled surface. The checking balance is shown prominently in the summary row and is read from `cash_accounts`. The gold Human Gate panel explains that agents may prepare an obligation but cannot release cash. When a ticket has an invoice, lease, or purchase target, the panel asks for the approver’s name (and a purchase amount where needed); approval then refreshes the balance and records the change. The gold treatment separates money decisions from routine ticket activity and makes the approval boundary hard to miss.

The visual language is intentionally warm: DM Sans keeps operational text readable, DM Mono gives IDs, statuses, and tool names a trustworthy instrument-panel feel, and a restrained Playfair Display accent makes the welcome message feel like a desk someone enjoys returning to. Teal communicates a stable workspace, coral highlights incoming work, violet marks machine/tool activity, and mint marks healthy or resolved states. Small live dots, subtle motion on active pulses, generous cards, and responsive breakpoints preserve that feeling on a laptop, tablet, or narrow screen. The result is a board that rewards a human’s attention while keeping every consequential action visible and grounded in the backend.
